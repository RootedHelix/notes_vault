A **truncated pyramid** is simply a pyramid where the **tip is cut off**.

Instead of the four glass panels meeting at a point, they stop and leave a **small flat opening at the top**.

So the geometry becomes:

```
      ______
     /      \
    /        \
   /          \
  /            \
 /              \
/________________\
```

Instead of:

```
      /\
     /  \
    /    \
   /      \
  /        \
 /          \
/____________\
```

### Why this improves the illusion

When the pyramid comes to a sharp point:

- reflections overlap
    
- the hologram looks compressed
    
- brightness drops near the top
    

With a **truncated pyramid (a frustum)**:

- reflections converge more cleanly
    
- the hologram appears **centered in the air**
    
- viewing angles improve
    
- the image looks more **three-dimensional**
    

This is why many **professional Pepper’s Ghost installations** use this geometry.

# The Small Optical Trick That Increases Brightness

This trick is **surprisingly simple**:

## Use Slightly Dark / Tinted Glass

Instead of perfectly clear glass, use **lightly smoked glass** (or a neutral density tint).

### Why it works

The hologram brightness stays the same, but the **background becomes darker**.

Human perception is based on **contrast**, not absolute brightness.

So:

```
hologram brightness / background brightness = perceived brightness
```

Example:

Without tint:

```
Hologram = 120 nits
Background reflections = 60 nits
Contrast = 2:1
```

With tint:

```
Hologram = 90 nits
Background reflections = 10 nits
Contrast = 9:1
```

The hologram suddenly looks **much brighter and more solid**, even though actual brightness dropped slightly.

This is used in many **museum holographic displays**.

# Another Important Trick (Almost Free)

Make the **inside of the enclosure completely black**.

Use:

- matte black paint
    
- flocking material
    
- black velvet
    

This absorbs stray light and dramatically improves the illusion.

Even small reflections inside the box can ruin hologram clarity.

# Why Your Display Choice Was Actually Smart

Your **1000-nit IPS panel** gives you headroom.

Even after:

- glass reflection loss
    
- tinting
    
- optical losses
    

the hologram should still appear bright.

Most hobby pyramids use **phone screens (300–400 nits)**, which is why they look dim.

# One Very Important Issue to Solve

When the screen is on top, **you must block stray light from escaping downward**.

Otherwise viewers see:

- direct screen glow
    
- reflections that break the illusion
    

Solution:

black shroud around the LCD

Think of a **hood around the screen** so only reflected light reaches the viewer.
